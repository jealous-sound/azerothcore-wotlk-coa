### Fixed

- Every Worldforged pickup now shows a prop in the world, and stands on the ground
  (`2026_09_22_03_worldforged_appearance_and_ground.sql`).
  - **Appearance.** 381 of the 1,742 pickup templates carried `displayId = 980926`, which the
    client's own `GameObjectDisplayInfo.dbc` resolves to `invisible_cube.mdx` - a placeholder.
    Such a pickup was a bare sparkle with nothing under it. Each one now shows a real model,
    taken from the archive's own captures rather than chosen by hand: 152 the model the realm
    uses for an object of the same name (a crate, a sack, a corpse), 76 the model the realm
    uses for the prop word in the name (a sack, a corpse, a ring, a totem, a fragment, a
    bracer), 139 the model the realm's own visible pickups use for an item of the same class
    and subclass (a necklace, boots, a bag, a gun rack), and 14 the container model the realm
    uses for its treasure chests. Every chosen id resolves to a real model in the client's own
    table, none is the placeholder, no size falls outside 0.6-1.5, and 156 distinct models are
    in use across the 381 objects.
  - **Height.** A pickup's position was recorded as X and Y only: a pin marks where a player
    stood when the loot window opened. The first pass took its heights from the community's
    zone dumps, which record the object's own stored height, and is left exactly as it is.
    Every later pass derived its height from whatever object stood nearest on the same map
    (2,836 placements), which left 1,465 of the world's 4,346 placements more than 10 yards off
    the ground - floating in the air or buried. Each later placement now takes its height from
    a first-pass placement of the same object within 60 yards where there is one (133, the very
    row the second pass copied, so those heights are measured), otherwise from the closest
    object the realm itself placed within 25 yards - the terrain surface when that object
    stands on the terrain, the floor it stands on when it does not, which is what keeps a
    pickup inside a mine, a cellar or a room where it belongs - and otherwise from the terrain
    surface the worldserver itself reads (`maps/*.map`, the same `GridTerrainData::getHeight`
    call the core makes). The 63 placements on maps with no terrain surface and the 94 with
    neither a surface nor anything nearby are left alone.
  - The rule was measured against the 1,510 first-pass heights, the only independent ground
    truth in the data: it reproduces 67% within 2 yards and 79% within 5 yards, better than the
    eight other variants tried, including "always the terrain surface" (64% and 74%). Applied,
    the later placements sit a median 0.00 yards from the terrain, 89% within 2 yards.
  - **Height, second reading** (`2026_09_22_04_worldforged_ground_refine.sql`). Re-read against
    every occupant of the world - the 95,665 authored objects and the 194,914 creatures it has
    always had - 69 rows move. Where three or more objects or creatures stand within 15 yards,
    agree with each other to within 20 yards and place the median more than 5 yards from the
    row, the row takes that median: the floor of the room, cave or terrace the pickup was
    looted on (34 rows). Where nothing at all stands within 15 yards and the row hangs between
    8 and 40 yards above the terrain, it floats in open air and takes the terrain (35 rows).
    The rows that match neither rule keep the height they were recorded with: above a
    heightfield a row more than 40 yards up is far more likely to stand on a structure the
    surface cannot see - a tree-city, a pier, a canopy - and a row below the terrain with
    nothing near it is what a cave or an undercroft looks like from above.
  - **Height, third reading** (`2026_09_22_05_worldforged_noheight_terrain.sql`). A map tile
    can carry one constant height for a whole grid instead of a surface
    (`MAP_HEIGHT_NO_HEIGHT`): `0` across an interior map such as Scarlet Monastery, the `-500`
    sentinel on a continent grid with no terrain under it. The core uses that constant as a
    fallback, so a pickup parked on it still loads - on a plane the map does not have, which is
    to say underground and out of sight. 43 such rows take back the height the pass that
    created them recorded. 15 rows a pass left at exactly `0` - the number a generator writes
    when nothing gave it a height, and not a height on any continent - take the terrain
    surface, 2 more take their recorded height, and 1 row the previous pass moved to an
    unsupported height is put back. 4 rows cannot be helped and stay at `0`: they are the
    Blackrock Caverns pickups, and that map has no tiles extracted in this repack and no
    creature or object of any kind standing on it, so nothing in the realm can say where its
    floor is.
  - After all seven passes: **3,498 placements stand on the terrain, 255 on a floor the
    realm's own occupants define, and 363 keep the height the data gives them - interiors,
    structures, and the four rows above.** Every display id in use - 1,072 distinct ids -
    resolves in the client's own `GameObjectDisplayInfo.dbc` to a model that exists in the
    client's archive chain, and none is the `980926` placeholder.
  - Appearance and height are read once at startup with the rest of the world, so a server
    restart is what makes the corrected placements visible.
- `mod-worldforged-pickups`'s README documents the pass, and its verification queries now cover
  the two defects (`SELECT COUNT(*) ... WHERE displayId=980926` is 0, and 0 for `displayId=0`).

### How to upgrade

Apply the migrations in `modules/mod-worldforged-pickups/data/sql/db-world/` in filename order -
`2026_09_22_03_worldforged_appearance_and_ground.sql`,
`2026_09_22_04_worldforged_ground_refine.sql` and
`2026_09_22_05_worldforged_noheight_terrain.sql` - or let the core's DB updater do it, then
restart the worldserver: both the appearance and the height of a placed object are read at
startup. Spawn rows are only ever read by `ObjectMgr::LoadGameobjects()` at boot, so a restart
is required - no reload command re-reads the `gameobject` table.
